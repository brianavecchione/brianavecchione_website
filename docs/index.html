I'm a Technical Researcher with the AI on the Ground team at the Data & Society Research Institute and a partner lead for Brown University's AI Research Institute on Interaction for AI Assistants (ARIA).

At Data & Society, I study technologies of health and care. Specifically, I research what happens when people turn to AI for socioemotional use—particularly with respect to mental health, therapeutic support, and the spectrum of AI companionship. At ARIA, I integrate empirical research and participatory methods to inform the landscape of support.

My work centers participatory and longitudinal methods to inform the design, evaluation, and regulation of AI for socioemotional use. This work spans several fields, including conversational AI, affective computing, and AI alignment. This orientation extends into my policy, advocacy, and community engagement work.

More broadly, my work centers on algorithmic auditing and institutional accountability. This includes foundational work on dataset transparency (Datasheets for Datasets, CACM 2021), the history and social justice dimensions of audit methodologies (Algorithmic Auditing and Social Justice, EAAMO 2021), and empirical studies of audit tooling gaps and regulatory compliance in practice — including work on New York City's algorithmic bias audit law (Auditing Work, FAccT 2024, Best Paper Award; Null Compliance, FAccT 2024).

I hold a Ph.D. in Information Science from Cornell University. My work has been supported by Google, Facebook (Meta), Microsoft, the National Science Foundation, the MacArthur Foundation, the Mozilla Foundation, and the Notre Dame–IBM Tech Ethics Lab, among others.
